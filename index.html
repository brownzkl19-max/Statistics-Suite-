📚 Learn Statistics

Mean

The mean is the average of a dataset. Add all values then divide by the number of values.


Example: 1,2,3,4,5
Mean = 3


Median

The median is the middle value after the data has been sorted.

Example: 1,2,3,4,5 Median = 3

Mode

The mode is the value that appears most often.

Example: 1,1,2,3,4 Mode = 1


Q1 (First Quartile)

Q1 is the value below which 25% of the data falls.


Q2 (Median)

Q2 is the middle point of the dataset. 50% of values lie below it.


Q3 (Third Quartile)

Q3 is the value below which 75% of the data falls.


IQR

IQR measures the spread of the middle 50% of data.

Formula: IQR = Q3 − Q1


Z-Score

Z-Score shows how many standard deviations a value is from the mean.

Positive = Above average
Negative = Below average


Percentile

A percentile tells you the percentage of data that falls below a value.

P90 means a value is higher than 90% of the dataset.


Outliers

Outliers are unusual values that are much higher or lower than the rest of the data.


Correlation

Correlation measures the strength of the relationship between two variables.

r = 1 → Perfect Positive
r = 0 → No Relationship
r = -1 → Perfect Negative


Regression

Regression predicts a value using another variable.

Statistics Formula Library

Review common introductory statistics formulas and what they mean.

Mean

x̄ = Σx / n

Add observations and divide by their count.

Population variance

σ² = Σ(x − μ)² / N

Average squared distance from the population mean.

Sample variance

s² = Σ(x − x̄)² / (n − 1)

Uses n − 1 when a sample estimates population variability.

Standard deviation

σ = √σ² or s = √s²

The square root of variance, in the original data units.

Range

Maximum − Minimum

Difference between the largest and smallest observation.

Z-score

z = (x − μ) / σ

How many standard deviations an observation is from the mean.

Interquartile range

IQR = Q3 − Q1

Spread of the middle 50% of ordered observations.

Permutation

nPr = n! / (n − r)!

Counts arrangements when order matters.

Combination

nCr = n! / [r!(n − r)!]

Counts selections when order does not matter.

Correlation

r = Σ[(x − x̄)(y − ȳ)] / √[Σ(x − x̄)²Σ(y − ȳ)²]

Describes direction and strength of a linear relationship.

Linear regression

ŷ = a + bx

Estimates y using an intercept and slope.

Coefficient of determination

R² = r²

For simple linear regression, variation explained by the linear model.

Notation and quartile methods can vary by course. Follow the method required by your instructor or research design.

Help & Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to help you use the platform.

How do I enter data into a calculator?

Enter numerical values separated by commas, spaces, or new lines. Check that the values are numbers before calculating.

What is the difference between population and sample variance?

Population variance divides the sum of squared deviations by N. Sample variance divides by n − 1 and is commonly used to estimate population variance from a sample.

Why does the mode sometimes say there is no mode?

If every value appears only once, there is no most frequent value, so the dataset has no mode under this calculator's definition.

How do I use the CSV Data Explorer?

Open CSV Data Explorer, choose a CSV file, indicate whether its first row contains column names, and select a detected numeric column to view its summary and distribution.

Are my uploaded CSV files sent to a server?

No. The CSV Data Explorer reads the selected file in your browser for the analysis shown on this page. Avoid using a shared or public device for sensitive data.

Where is my calculation history stored?

History is saved in local storage in your current browser. It does not automatically sync between devices, and you can clear it from the History section.

Can I use Statistics Suite on my phone?

Yes. The layout adapts to smaller screens and includes a mobile navigation bar. For the best experience, use an up-to-date browser.

Are the results a substitute for checking my work?

No. Use the results as a learning aid and verify important academic or research calculations against your course materials and required statistical methods.
